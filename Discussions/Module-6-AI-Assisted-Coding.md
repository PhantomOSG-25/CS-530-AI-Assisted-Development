# Module 6 Discussion: Ethics and Risks of AI-Assisted Coding

This discussion focused on the ethical and practical risks of using AI-generated code.

My position throughout the discussion was that AI can be useful, but responsibility remains with the developer and organization using the output. A major example came from the earlier debugging activity, where GitHub Copilot confidently described invalid Python syntax as valid until I challenged it with evidence from Python and VS Code.

The discussion also covered:

- Security risks in AI-generated code.
- Intellectual-property and copyright concerns.
- Human review and accountability.
- The danger of treating a human reviewer as a rubber stamp.
- The need for reviewers to have enough knowledge, information, time, and authority to reject or escalate AI output.

Two peer responses used outside sources to expand the discussion. One used Pearce et al. (2022) on security risks in Copilot-generated code, and another used the U.S. Copyright Office's 2025 report on AI and copyrightability.

Professor Francis Akowuah also asked what organizational practices would make human review genuine rather than symbolic. My response emphasized that reviewers need the knowledge, time, information, and authority to challenge AI output, and that company pressure for quick approval can turn supposed oversight into simple rubber-stamping.
