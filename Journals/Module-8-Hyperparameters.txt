(To see the reader’s view of your document, click on the View tab then select Reading View.)A hyperparameter is a setting chosen by the people building or using an AI model rather than something the model learns for itself. I think of it as a control setting that affects how the system learns or how it produces an answer. During training, examples include the learning rate, batch size, and number of training passes. After a model is trained, generation settings such as temperature and top-p control how predictable or varied its output will be. These choices matter because changing the settings can change the quality, consistency, and fairness of the results.

Heading OneHow Hyperparameters Affect Training
Heading levels 1-3 get their own paragraph, as shown. Headings 4 and 5 are run-in headings used at the beginning of the paragraph. Include a period at the end of a run-in heading. Double-space all text, including headings. Use descriptive headings to help readers identify sections of your paper.Training hyperparameters control the learning process. The learning rate determines how large of an adjustment the model makes as it learns from mistakes. If it is too high, the model can make changes that are too large and fail to settle on a good solution. If it is too low, training can become very slow or fail to improve enough. Batch size controls how many training examples are processed before an update is made, and the number of epochs controls how many times the model works through the training data. Too little training can leave the model underprepared, while too much can cause it to learn the training examples too closely and perform worse on new situations.

They found that different combinations produced very different results, including no output, code that would not run, code that failed unit tests, and correct functional code. Their results showed stronger code-generation performance with temperature below 0.5 and top-p below 0.75, along with specific ranges for the penalty settings. This is important because it shows that the same model can produce better or worse code depending on how it is configured.
Heading ThreeImpact on Bias and Ethics
When citing any quote or text that consists of three or more lines, APA guidelines call for block-quote format:Accuracy is only part of the issue. Hyperparameter choices can also change the kinds of responses a model is more likely to produce. Dhamala et al. (2023) studied temperature, top-p, and top-k settings and found that fairness across demographic groups changed significantly when the decoding settings changed. They also found a tradeoff where settings that produced more diverse text could produce more negative sentiment or negative regard toward some groups. That does not mean one setting is automatically ethical and another is unethical, but it does mean the settings can change the likelihood of biased output.

To correctly format a block-quote, indent each line of the text to one-half inch. Remember to always cite your source.For code generation, bias can show up in more than the wording of an answer. A model may perform better for one programming language, framework, naming style, or type of problem than another because of differences in training data and model behavior. If developers only tune for speed or average accuracy, they may miss cases where one group of users or one type of input receives worse results. Hyperparameters cannot remove bias that is already present in the training data, but poor tuning can make the problem harder to notice or can increase how often problematic outputs appear.
